The thesis

Zoning is the unpriced variable in every land deal.

Two identical parcels, one street apart. One is entitled; the other needs a variance that takes nineteen months. The market prices them nearly the same — until it doesn't.

Entitlement delay is the quiet deal-killer in commercial real estate. A rezoning application stalls in committee, a neighborhood plan gets rewritten, a use table changes under a signed LOI. The land value doesn't move on a spreadsheet — it evaporates in a hearing room. Firms that map this exposure early underwrite with precision; everyone else discovers it at closing.

The other side of risk is return. Rezonings mint fortunes: a parcel flipped from industrial to mixed-use, a corridor upzoned along a transit line, a municipality hungry for housing that rewrites its code overnight. Zoning changes create the steepest value inflections in real estate — and they are public, legible, and predictable to anyone watching the right signals.

ZoningRisk.com names the entire discipline. It is the brand a data platform, an insurer, or a consultancy builds on when the product is the difference between what a parcel is zoned for and what it could become.
