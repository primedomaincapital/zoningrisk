What zoning risk costs, in months and dollars

Entitlement timelines, rezoning costs, and the regulatory share of a new home's price, from builder surveys and published municipal schedules.

A developer's worst line item is rarely concrete. It is time: months spent waiting on a rezoning vote while carrying costs compound, and a fee schedule that only reveals its full size once studies pile up. The national survey data puts a number on the burden — more than a quarter of a new home's price now traces to regulation at some level of government — while county schedules and practitioner accounts show how unevenly that burden lands from one jurisdiction to the next.

How to read these numbers

Included: the NAHB builder survey series (2011–2026), a published California county rezoning schedule, and practitioner guides that state their own limits. Excluded: individual project pro formas, paywalled entitlement databases, and any figure presented as a national average when its source covers one market only. The Arlington figure is kept precisely because its author warns against generalizing from it.
